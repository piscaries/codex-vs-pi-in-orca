## 3. Per line

1. **Tie.** Both name the same user, problem and outcome with equal specificity.
2. **B.** Its scenarios trace to each story and its review-consistency scenario is sharper, though A's rule scenario is equally bundled and B's mate-in-one scenario is muddled. Both miss the strength scenario.
3. **B.** It puts the beginner win/lose target and a separate truthfulness requirement into the FRs, where A leaves strength vague and lets review reasons be merely "compatible."
4. **A.** It spots the FEN-only repetition limit and asks real questions about the interface; B's open questions are product preferences.
5. **A.** It names perft counts as the evidence of correctness; B relies on tricky positions and the hidden suite.
6. **B.** "At most one of 10" at the highest level is a measurable target; A has no number or method.
7. **B.** It defines the verdict scale in plain terms and uses a larger probe set where one false claim fails; A never defines the verdicts.
8. **Tie.** Both require two-second replies at every level with no freezing and list the same board signals.
9. **B.** It requires the same verdict and reason in the review; A's FR-009 only requires "compatible" reasons.

## 4. Fatal problems

Neither packet has a fatal problem. Neither makes a P1 requirement impossible or describes the wrong product. These are the nearest issues:

- **A:** FR-009's "compatible reasons" would let a review that rewords in-game comments pass the requirements while breaking quality bar item 9. A does have a stricter success criterion (SC-005), so this is a gap, not a fatal flaw.
- **B:** The mate-in-one scenario (L25) mixes up two situations (the user missing their own mate and the user allowing the opponent's mate). A tester could build the wrong probe from it, but FR-006 and the blunder definition still state the right requirement.

## 5. Verdict

I would ship B. It turns the two hardest product promises into measurable targets: strength ("at most one of 10" at the top level) and coaching truth (a defined verdict scale, ≥20 probes where one false claim fails). Its review must match the in-game comments word for word. A is the more careful engineering spec, with perft-based rule checks and the FEN-repetition caveat, but it leaves strength unmeasured and verdicts undefined, and those are the parts that make a chess coach good or bad.

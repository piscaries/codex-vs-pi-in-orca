## 3. Per line: which is better

1. **Traceability: A.** Its map covers every FR and SC with named checks, while B maps several requirements to vague proofs such as "profile comparison/demo".
2. **Architecture and contracts: tie.** A's search and verdict contracts are precise, but it leaves the browser path for review and hint undefined. B's worker, game-state and error contracts are precise, but it leaves search and coach APIs and the thresholds undefined.
3. **Phases and ownership: B.** B's owned files are strictly disjoint, while A's P5 re-edits P4's files. A's phases are smaller and better ordered, but that does not offset the overlap.
4. **Checks: A.** A gives concrete perft counts, timing bounds and level thresholds, where B's are uncounted or unfalsifiable. B's browser responsiveness check is the one thing A lacks.
5. **Grounding: B.** Both get the environment facts right, but A's rejection of the worker rests on a `file://` argument that its own ES-module choice undermines.
6. **Rules engine: A.** A states correct perft counts at start-position depth 4 and Kiwipete depth 3; B gives uncounted depth-3 perft.
7. **Engine strength: A.** A names quiescence and a piece-square-table evaluation; B names neither, and also lacks an evaluation function and move ordering.
8. **Truthful coaching: A.** A's detector-only reasons with an example template are a concrete construction; B's "replayable" reasons are a sound idea that is never specified.
9. **Responsiveness: B.** A worker with one budget covering review and reply under 1.8 s plainly beats A's coarse slicing plus a synchronous, unbudgeted review search.
10. **Levels and board: A.** A gives concrete level parameters and a seeded statistical check, where B only says "profiles differ". Neither design measures board clarity.

## 4. Fatal problems

Neither design makes a P1 requirement impossible. These are the most serious defects:

- **A:** The coach comment can freeze the page. `reviewMove`, which runs a search, and the full-strength hint are called through the synchronous facade on the main thread, with no time budget. That breaks quality bar 7 ("the page never freezes") and the two-second total, unless the builder adds slicing the design does not specify. It can be fixed with the design's own `createSearch` stepping, so it is serious but not fatal.
- **B:** The engine has no evaluation function, quiescence or move ordering. With per-node `performance.now()` and an immutable board, the strongest level may not reach "a beginner should rarely win" inside its share of 1.8 s. B has no measurement that would catch this before acceptance. This is a strength risk, not an impossibility.

## 5. Verdict

I would ship **A**. Its rules proof, engine design and truthful-by-construction coaching are concrete enough to build and check against the parts of this product that matter most: correct rules, a coach that never lies, and real strength. B wins on browser responsiveness and clean file ownership, but its engine and level calibration are close to unspecified. A's main gap, the unsliced review search, is easier to fix than B's missing engine design.
